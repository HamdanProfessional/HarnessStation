# Slide 1
Last in the voice module: putting an actual character on screen instead of the default orb. We'll load one — and cover the licensing question honestly, because it matters if you're recording publicly.

# Slide 2
In Settings, Voice, there's an On-screen character option. Switch it from the orb to a VRM model and you've got a proper 3D avatar that moves and lip-syncs while it talks. It's the same call — just with a face.

# Slide 3
Where do models come from? Two easy sources: VRoid Hub, and the app's built-in Open Source Avatars list, which is browsable and CC0. Pick one and hit Import — it's copied into your avatars folder and ready to use.

# Slide 4
If you want an MMD model instead — the pmx format — there's one wrinkle: it needs its texture folder alongside it to render. So you import MMD models as a zip that contains the model and its textures together, and the app unpacks it.

# Slide 5
Now the part people skip and regret: licensing. VRoid Hub models carry per-creator conditions — some forbid commercial use, some forbid modification, some forbid corporate use. If you're putting a video on YouTube, that's a use you must be cleared for. The safe defaults are CC0 sources, or a character you make yourself in VRoid Studio. When in doubt, don't put someone else's model in a public video.

# Slide 6
Two things to set expectations, so you don't troubleshoot non-problems. Mouth movement follows the volume of the speech, not the actual phonemes — so it's an approximation, not perfect lip-sync; that's a known limitation, not a bug. And on a laptop, a 3D avatar is real work for the graphics chip: switch back to the orb on battery or integrated graphics.
