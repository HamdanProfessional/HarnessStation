# Slide 1
Once you have real history, you need to manage it. Folders, snapshots, branching, and compaction — the tools for a chat list that has grown past a handful of entries.

# Slide 2
Right-click any chat: pin it, rename it or leave auto-title running, move it to a folder, duplicate it, export as Markdown or JSON, or delete it. Delete has no undo — that is the one to pause on.

# Slide 3
Before a risky tool-using task — one that edits files or runs commands — take a snapshot. If it goes wrong, restore it. The chat keeps its identity through a restore; it is the same chat rolled back, not a new one.

# Slide 4
Branch and edit look similar but do different jobs. Branch from a message forks a new chat and leaves the original untouched — good for exploring an alternative without losing the first path. Edit a message changes history in place, in the same chat — good when you just got the wording wrong.

# Slide 5
Compact shows a banner and folds older turns down to a summary, keeping recent messages verbatim. Turn on auto-compact with a threshold and it happens on its own once a chat crosses a length you set, instead of waiting for you to notice.

# Slide 6
Gotcha: compaction loses detail on purpose — that is the whole mechanism, not a bug. Anything you will want verbatim later belongs in an export or in memory before it gets folded, not left to survive compaction on its own.
