# Slide 1
Module 10 is automation. First up, workflows — a way to chain steps so one step's output feeds the next, instead of one long prompt trying to do everything at once.

# Slide 2
Go to Automation, Workflows, New workflow. A workflow is a fixed sequence of steps, each one its own prompt, its own tools, even its own model, run in order with each step's output handed to the next.

# Slide 3
The example: a weekly repo summary in three steps. Step one, Gather, pulls the week's commits and issues with a tool-using model. Step two, Group, sorts that raw list into themes. Step three, Write, turns the grouped themes into a readable summary. Each step is small and does one job well.

# Slide 4
Run it, and watch each step's output as it lands. That's worth doing at least once — seeing exactly what Gather handed to Group, and what Group handed to Write, is how you catch a step quietly going wrong before it reaches the end.

# Slide 5
Two reliability habits worth building in from the start. Tell early steps not to summarize — say “return the output unchanged” — so nothing gets quietly trimmed before the step that's actually meant to condense it. And have steps signal failure explicitly: “if no results, reply exactly: NO DATA” gives the next step something unambiguous to check for.

# Slide 6
The gotcha: a workflow only works for a known shape — a sequence you can name in advance. If the next step depends on what gets discovered along the way, a fixed sequence fights you. That's the job for a chat with tools, which can decide its next move as it goes.

# Slide 7
That raises the bigger question — agent, workflow, or chat: which one for which job? Next episode is a short reference you can jump straight to.
