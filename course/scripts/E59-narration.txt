# Slide 1
Module 15 is about precise context control. First up: rewindTo — stepping a chat back to an earlier point without leaving the chat you're in.

# Slide 2
Pick a point earlier in the conversation and rewind to it. Everything after that point is gone from the chat — not archived off to the side, actually removed — and you're back at that moment, ready to go a different direction.

# Slide 3
Here's the safety net: rewindTo snapshots the chat before it deletes anything. So a rewind is itself reversible — if you rewind too far, or change your mind, you can get back what was there. It behaves like an undo, not a shredder.

# Slide 4
Compare this to Branch from here, from episode forty-six. Branching preserves the original conversation and forks a brand new chat from that point — you end up with two chats. Rewind does the opposite: it mutates the current chat back to an earlier state, in place. Same starting idea, opposite trade-off.

# Slide 5
Use rewind when you want a clean redo without a second chat cluttering your list. Use branch when you want to keep both paths side by side and compare them. They solve the same itch — a wrong turn — in two different ways.

# Slide 6
Rewind operates on whole turns. Next episode goes finer-grained still: deleting a single reasoning block, tool call, or tool response without touching anything else.
