# Slide 1
Let's talk about getting genuinely better results — the handful of prompt patterns that measurably help a tool-using agent. I'll show before-and-after on camera so you can see the difference, not just take my word for it.

# Slide 2
Three that pay off immediately. Say what you don't want — “skip style comments, only real bugs.” Give it explicit permission to find nothing — that single line kills padded, low-signal answers. And ask it to show its evidence or quote the source — the best defense against confident invention.

# Slide 3
Three more about how you drive it. Ask for a plan before any multi-file work — cheaper to fix a plan than an edit. Work in small steps instead of one giant ask. And tell it what to do when stuck: ask you, or make an assumption and flag it — so it doesn't silently guess.

# Slide 4
And what doesn't help, so you can stop wasting words on it: politeness, “you are a senior engineer” role-play framing, very long instructions, and — importantly — repeating an instruction it already ignored. None of these measurably improve a modern tool-using model.

# Slide 5
That last one deserves a gotcha. Repeating an ignored instruction almost never works, because the problem usually isn't emphasis — it's that something else in the prompt is contradicting it. Go back to the layering model from Episode 11 and find the conflict instead of shouting louder.
