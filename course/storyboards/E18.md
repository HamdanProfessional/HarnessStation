## Slide 2 · ~9s

**Narration:** In Settings, Voice, there's an On-screen character option. Switch it from the orb to a VRM model and you've got a proper 3D avatar that moves and lip-syncs while it talks. It's the same call — just with a face.

**On screen / action:** SWITCH TO APP: Settings › Voice › On-screen character; switch from Orb to a VRM; show it in a call.

## Slide 3 · ~11s

**Narration:** Where do models come from? Two easy sources: VRoid Hub, and the app's built-in Open Source Avatars list, which is browsable and CC0. Pick one and hit Import — it's copied into your avatars folder and ready to use.

**On screen / action:** APP: use the in-app Open Source Avatars (CC0) list; pick one and click Import. Mention VRoid Hub too.

## Slide 4 · ~9s

**Narration:** If you want an MMD model instead — the pmx format — there's one wrinkle: it needs its texture folder alongside it to render. So you import MMD models as a zip that contains the model and its textures together, and the app unpacks it.

## Slide 5 · ~14s

**Narration:** Now the part people skip and regret: licensing. VRoid Hub models carry per-creator conditions — some forbid commercial use, some forbid modification, some forbid corporate use. If you're putting a video on YouTube, that's a use you must be cleared for. The safe defaults are CC0 sources, or a character you make yourself in VRoid Studio. When in doubt, don't put someone else's model in a public video.

## Slide 6 · ~12s

**Narration:** Two things to set expectations, so you don't troubleshoot non-problems. Mouth movement follows the volume of the speech, not the actual phonemes — so it's an approximation, not perfect lip-sync; that's a known limitation, not a bug. And on a laptop, a 3D avatar is real work for the graphics chip: switch back to the orb on battery or integrated graphics.
