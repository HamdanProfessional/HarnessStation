## Slide 1 · ~6s

**Narration:** Two habits to close the tools module: letting the model switch on tools it needs by itself, and reading tool-call cards to understand exactly what happened.

## Slide 2 · ~13s

**Narration:** In Settings, General, there's an option to let the model switch on tools it finds itself. When on, if a task needs a capability, it can enable it mid-conversation instead of you doing it manually. Handy — but note the boundary.

**On screen / action:** SWITCH TO APP: Settings › General; toggle 'let the model switch on tools it finds itself'.

## Slide 3 · ~11s

**Narration:** The important boundary: it can turn on tools that need no credentials — file search, web fetch, and the like — but it cannot connect anything that needs your API key or a sign-in. So it can help itself to safe capabilities, but it can never quietly wire up something that spends your money or touches an account. That line is deliberate.

## Slide 4 · ~11s

**Narration:** Second habit: read the cards. When a result surprises you, expand the tool-call card and look at the exact arguments the model used. Nine times out of ten the surprise is in there — it searched for the wrong term, or read the wrong file. The answer to “why did it do that” is usually one expand away.

**On screen / action:** APP: expand a tool-call card mid-conversation; point at the exact arguments and the result.

## Slide 5 · ~11s

**Narration:** And the theme that runs through everything from here on, worth stating flatly: forty enabled tools choose worse than five. More options make the model's job harder, not easier. Keep each chat's toolset tight. We'll come back to this with agents, workflows, and cost — it's the single most repeated lesson in the course.
