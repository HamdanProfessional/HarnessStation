# E60 · Deleting individual items from context

## Slide 1 · ~8s

**Narration:** Rewind steps back through whole turns. This episode is finer-grained: deleting a single reasoning block, a single tool call, or a single tool response, without touching the rest of the chat.

## Slide 2 · ~12s

**Narration:** Three controls, all triggered by hovering. Hover a reasoning block and you get “Delete this thinking from context.” Hover a tool call and you get “Delete this tool call from context.” Hover its response and you get “Delete this tool response from context.” Each targets exactly one item.

**On screen / action:** APP: in a chat with tool use, hover a reasoning block, a tool call, and its response — show all three delete controls.

## Slide 3 · ~13s

**Narration:** Why this exists: a long tool-using session accumulates noisy or flat-out wrong intermediate steps. That's poisoning the model's context — it keeps referring back to a bad grep or a stale file read — but the thread as a whole is still worth keeping. Full compaction is too blunt, and starting over throws away everything good.

## Slide 4 · ~12s

**Narration:** So surgical deletion beats the two blunt alternatives. Instead of starting over and losing the whole thread, or running a full compaction that summarizes everything, you remove exactly the items that are wrong or irrelevant, and leave the rest of the context untouched.

## Slide 5 · ~10s

**Narration:** And the direct payoff: fewer tokens. Every deleted reasoning block, tool call, or tool response is context the model no longer has to read on the next turn — a smaller, cleaner prompt, and a cheaper one.

## Slide 6 · ~10s

**Narration:** Gotcha: these controls are disabled while a reply is streaming. Deletion only applies to settled history — you can't reach in and edit context mid-generation.
