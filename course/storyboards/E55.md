## Slide 1 · ~7s

**Narration:** A full spoken exchange with no server behind it. This episode covers how the browser build hears you and how it talks back, entirely client-side.

## Slide 2 · ~12s

**Narration:** Click the mic and mic dot ts captures your voice with getUserMedia, no plugin, no native permission dialog — just the browser's own microphone prompt. By default the browser build hands that audio straight to the browser's own built-in speech recognition — fast, and nothing to download.

**On screen / action:** SWITCH TO APP: web build, click the mic, speak a short prompt, watch it transcribe.

## Slide 3 · ~12s

**Narration:** If that's not available — an older browser, a locked-down one — it falls back to the same record-then-transcribe path as desktop: a 16 kilohertz WAV written into OPFS, transcribed client-side with transformers dot js Whisper. Same behavior, one extra step.

## Slide 4 · ~12s

**Narration:** For the reply, speak dot ts gives you three options: Kokoro, which is already WASM so it just runs; any cloud voice engine you've configured; or the browser's own SpeechSynthesis as the built-in, always-available fallback.

**On screen / action:** APP: play a spoken reply — show the voice engine picker with Kokoro selected.

## Slide 5 · ~10s

**Narration:** Put it together and it's a full spoken exchange — ask a question out loud, hear the answer back — in a plain tab, with no Tauri process, no native app, nothing installed.

## Slide 6 · ~9s

**Narration:** Worth remembering: browser SpeechRecognition quality and language coverage varies by browser and OS. If it feels off, Whisper is still there as the consistent, model-based fallback.
