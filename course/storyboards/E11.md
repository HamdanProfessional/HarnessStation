# E11 · The agent loop

## Slide 1 · ~6s

**Narration:** Time for the mental model that explains almost every “why did it do that” moment for the rest of the series: what actually happens when you send a single turn.

## Slide 2 · ~12s

**Narration:** Here's the loop. The app assembles a prompt and sends it, along with the list of tools, to the model. The model replies with either text or a tool call. If it's a tool call, the app runs it, adds the result, and goes round again. If it's text, the turn stops. That's the entire engine.

## Slide 3 · ~11s

**Narration:** The consequence that surprises people: every trip around that loop is a separate, separately-billed request to the model. A task that makes eight tool calls is nine requests, not one. That's why an agentic task costs far more than a single question — and why it's worth being specific so it doesn't wander.

## Slide 4 · ~14s

**Narration:** What actually goes into that prompt, in order? Global instructions, then project instructions, then this chat's instructions, then any recalled memory, then retrieved knowledge, then the skill index, then the tool definitions, then the conversation history — and your new message goes last. Knowing this order explains a lot about what the model pays attention to.

## Slide 5 · ~12s

**Narration:** And why does the loop ever stop? Five reasons: it answered you; it hit the step limit; it caught itself repeating unproductively; you stopped it; a spend cap kicked in; or it errored. If a task ends earlier than you expected, it's one of these — usually the step limit or a repeat.

## Slide 6 · ~12s

**Narration:** One gotcha that saves hours of confusion. Those instruction layers add up — they don't replace each other. A chat prompt does not override the global one; they're all present at once. So the commonest cause of an ignored instruction is a contradiction between two layers. When something's ignored, look for what's fighting it, not for a bug.
