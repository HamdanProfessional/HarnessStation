## Slide 4 · ~11s

**Narration:** Here is the catch. These test questions are, at this point, plastered across the internet. Training data for newer models likely includes material that looks a lot like the benchmark itself — so a high score partly reflects memorization, not raw capability.

## Slide 5 · ~11s

**Narration:** Which makes a benchmark a weak proxy for your actual task. A model that tops a coding leaderboard can still fumble the exact tool-calling pattern your workflow depends on. Use benchmarks to narrow the field — three or four candidates — never to make the final call.

## Slide 6 · ~10s

**Narration:** So the rule: shortlist with Benchmarks, then test with Evals — your own test cases, on your own task. That is next episode, and it is the step that actually tells you which model wins for you.
