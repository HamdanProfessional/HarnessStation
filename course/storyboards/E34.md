## Slide 1 · ~7s

**Narration:** Module 10 is automation. First up, workflows — a way to chain steps so one step's output feeds the next, instead of one long prompt trying to do everything at once.

## Slide 2 · ~10s

**Narration:** Go to Automation, Workflows, New workflow. A workflow is a fixed sequence of steps, each one its own prompt, its own tools, even its own model, run in order with each step's output handed to the next.

**On screen / action:** SWITCH TO APP: Automation › Workflows › New workflow.

## Slide 3 · ~13s

**Narration:** The example: a weekly repo summary in three steps. Step one, Gather, pulls the week's commits and issues with a tool-using model. Step two, Group, sorts that raw list into themes. Step three, Write, turns the grouped themes into a readable summary. Each step is small and does one job well.

**On screen / action:** APP: build the three-step workflow — Gather, Group, Write — naming tools and model per step.

## Slide 4 · ~11s

**Narration:** Run it, and watch each step's output as it lands. That's worth doing at least once — seeing exactly what Gather handed to Group, and what Group handed to Write, is how you catch a step quietly going wrong before it reaches the end.

**On screen / action:** APP: run the workflow; open each step's output as it completes.

## Slide 5 · ~12s

**Narration:** Two reliability habits worth building in from the start. Tell early steps not to summarize — say “return the output unchanged” — so nothing gets quietly trimmed before the step that's actually meant to condense it. And have steps signal failure explicitly: “if no results, reply exactly: NO DATA” gives the next step something unambiguous to check for.

## Slide 6 · ~12s

**Narration:** The gotcha: a workflow only works for a known shape — a sequence you can name in advance. If the next step depends on what gets discovered along the way, a fixed sequence fights you. That's the job for a chat with tools, which can decide its next move as it goes.
