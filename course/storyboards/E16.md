## Slide 1 · ~6s

**Narration:** Now the speaking half. There are four synthesis engines to choose from, and they trade off quality, cost, privacy, and language. Let's compare them and land on a sensible default.

## Slide 2 · ~14s

**Narration:** Here's the lineup. Kokoro: free, local, genuinely good — but English only. Cloud: the best quality, priced per character, from OpenAI, ElevenLabs, Cartesia or Groq. Piper: free and local too, lighter and faster but more synthetic. System: your OS voice — free, instant, flattest, but often the only offline option for other languages. And Auto, which just picks for you.

## Slide 3 · ~12s

**Narration:** Let's hear Kokoro — the one I'd default to. First use downloads about ninety megabytes, once. And a nice detail: it generates speech a sentence at a time, so a long reply starts talking almost immediately instead of making you wait for the whole thing.

**On screen / action:** SWITCH TO APP: Settings › Voice; select Kokoro; play a preview. Mention the ~90MB one-time download.

## Slide 4 · ~10s

**Narration:** If you want the very best delivery, plug in a cloud engine with a key. The app handles a failed request gracefully — if the cloud call fails mid-reply, it falls back rather than going silent, so a call never just dies on you.

**On screen / action:** APP: switch to a cloud engine, paste a key; trigger a reply; (optional) show fallback if a request fails.

## Slide 5 · ~10s

**Narration:** The gotcha to remember: Kokoro and Piper are English only. If you need another language, you've got two routes — a cloud engine that supports it, or your system voice with that language pack installed. Don't fight the local engines to speak a language they simply don't have.
